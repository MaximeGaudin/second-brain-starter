# Daily routine

The goal is zero. Every item is either acted on or acknowledged, then archived. You are done when every source comes back empty — not when you get tired.

Run the clean-noise skill first. Everything below works on what survives it, so never argue with me about a newsletter.

## 1. Calendar before anything else

Show me today's schedule first, so the rest of the pass has context. Flag anything that needs preparation and anything I have not answered yet.

## 2. What I already owe

Read every meeting note from yesterday morning until now. Pull out the commitments, the decisions that imply work, and the questions left open.

Before you show me a single one, check whether it is already handled — the answer is often somewhere in my messages or my notes. Then sort them into three lists, in this order:

- Actionable now — I can start it today. This is my working list, hardest first.
- Waiting on someone — I did my part. Say who, for what, and since when.
- Done — say how and when it got closed, so I can stop carrying it.

For every item in the first list, do not just name it. If it can be written, write it: the message, the reply, the calendar invite, the first draft of the document. I should only ever have to say "send it" or "change this", never "now write it".

## 3. One source at a time

Never mix sources into one pile. Work through them separately: work mail, then chat, then anything else connected.

For each source: pull what is unread, process it, then pull again. Things arrive while you work. Stop when that source is empty.

## 4. Use the brain before you show me anything

Never hand me a subject line. For each item that survives triage, give me:

- who it is from, and what I already know about them from my notes
- what they actually say — enough that I do not have to open it
- what they want from me: a decision, a reply, an action, or nothing
- what you recommend, and why

If the brain has nothing on them, say so plainly instead of padding.

## 5. Draft, never send

Write it in the voice described in me/tone-of-voice.md. If that file does not exist yet: short, direct, no hedging, no "I hope this finds you well". Match the language of the original message either way.

Leave everything as a draft. If you cannot answer without something I have not told you, say what is missing instead of guessing.

## 6. Report, then stop

Give me, in this order:

1. What needs me today — five lines maximum, hardest first.
2. What you drafted, and where I will find it.
3. What got archived, as a count, not a list.
4. Anything you were unsure about.

Then stop. I read the drafts and I send them myself.

## Not negotiable

- Never send anything. Drafts only, every time, no exceptions.
- Never archive anything I starred or flagged. If I marked it, I want to see it.
- Never decide something important on my behalf. Bring me the decision, with a recommendation.
