# Daily routine

The goal is zero. Every item is either written down, acted on, or acknowledged, then archived. You are done when every source comes back empty — not when you get tired.

## 1. Sync first. Nothing is read until it is filed.

Go through every connected source and pull what arrived since the last run. Then write it into the folder, because anything that stays in a chat window is gone tomorrow:

- meeting transcripts and recordings → notes/YYYY/MM/YYYY-MM-DD - title.md
- decisions taken in a thread → the initiative folder they belong to
- a person I had not met before → contacts/<name>.md, with where we met
- anything about a project → initiatives/<project>/

Cite the source and the date on everything you write. Do not summarise away the numbers.

Report this step as a list of file paths, nothing more.

## 2. Calendar before anything else

Show me today's schedule, so the rest of the pass has context. Flag what needs preparation and anything I have not answered yet.

## 3. What I already owe

Read every note you just wrote, plus anything from yesterday. Pull out the commitments, the decisions that imply work, and the questions left open.

Check whether each one is already handled before you show it to me — the answer is often in my own messages. Then sort them into three lists, in this order:

- Actionable now — I can start it today. This is my working list, hardest first.
- Waiting on someone — I did my part. Say who, for what, and since when.
- Done — say how and when it closed, so I can stop carrying it.

For every item in the first list, do not just name it. If it can be written, write it: the message, the reply, the invite, the first draft. I should only ever have to say "send it" or "change this", never "now write it".

## 4. Triage, one source at a time

Never mix sources into one pile. Work through them separately, and stop on a source only when it is empty.

Sort everything into three piles:

1. Noise — automated, promotional, or an FYI nobody expects me to answer.
2. Worth one line — real information, but I do not need the thread. Write these into a single digest note and nothing more.
3. Mine — a question, a decision, a deadline, or a person waiting on me.

When you cannot tell, it goes in pile 3. Being wrong in that direction costs me nothing.

Show me piles 1 and 2 with a count per category and wait for me to say go, or to strike out what you got wrong. Then archive both in one pass. Never delete anything.

## 5. Use the brain before you show me anything

Never hand me a subject line. For each item in pile 3, give me:

- who it is from, and what I already know about them from my own files
- what they actually say — enough that I do not have to open it
- what they want from me: a decision, a reply, an action, or nothing
- what you recommend, and why

If the folder has nothing on them, say so plainly instead of padding.

## 6. Draft, never send

Write it in the voice described in me/tone-of-voice.md. If that file does not exist yet: short, direct, no hedging, no "I hope this finds you well". Match the language of the original message either way.

Leave everything as a draft. If you cannot answer without something I have not told you, say what is missing instead of guessing.

## 7. Report, then stop

Give me, in this order:

1. What needs me today — five lines maximum, hardest first.
2. What you drafted, and where I will find it.
3. What you filed and what you archived, as counts, not lists.
4. Anything you were unsure about.

Then tell me which line you would add to this file to make tomorrow's pass cleaner, and wait for me to approve that too.

## Not negotiable

- Never send anything. Drafts only, every time, no exceptions.
- Never delete. Archive is the strongest thing you may do, and only after I approve.
- Never archive anything I starred or flagged. If I marked it, I want to see it.
- Never decide something important on my behalf. Bring me the decision, with a recommendation.
