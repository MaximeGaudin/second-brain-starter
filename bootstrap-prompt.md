  me/            who I am, my role, my goals

STEP 2 — Write the rules into README.md

Create README.md at the root of the folder. It documents the structure above, and these rules:

  - Plain markdown only. No database, no app, no export format. The folder is the product and the editor is replaceable.
  - Notes are filed as notes/YYYY/MM/YYYY-MM-DD - title.md
  - Every initiative is a folder containing sources/ (raw material I did not write), knowledge/ (what we work out together) and output/ (what I actually ship).
  - Filenames are lowercase, hyphenated and readable by a human six months from now.
  - Cite the file every fact came from. If something is not in these files, say so instead of guessing.
  - Never send an email, message or document on my behalf. Draft it, show me, and let me press send.
  - When an answer is wrong, we fix the instruction that produced it, not the answer itself.

STEP 3 — Interview me

Ask me these three questions ONE AT A TIME. Wait for my answer before asking the next one. Do not answer them for me, and do not invent extra questions.

  1. Describe yourself in a few sentences — your role, the company you work for, and what you actually spend your days doing.
  2. Name a few colleagues you work with most often, and say what each of them owns.
  3. What are your objectives for this quarter?

Then, before you write anything, search the web for the company I named in answer 1. Find what it sells and who it sells to, its size and stage, its products, and anything public from the last twelve months. Where you cannot confirm something, mark it unverified rather than filling the gap.

Then write, from my answers and your research:

  me/context.md      — answers 1 and 3 in my own words, and nothing you inferred
  me/company.md      — what you found on the web, every claim followed by the URL it came from
  team/<name>.md     — one short file per person from answer 2, with what they own

Show me me/company.md before you move on. It is the only file here you wrote rather than I did, so I would rather correct it now than find it wrong in a month.

# AI Summit Barcelona 2026 — Build Your Second Brain

## The premise

A second brain is a plain folder of markdown files plus an AI agent that can read it. No database, no app, nothing to migrate. The folder is the product; the editor is replaceable. The value comes from what goes into the folder, not from the tool reading it.

## Setup, four steps

1. Download Cursor from cursor.com/download and install it
2. Sign in — your name in the bottom-left corner is the confirmation
3. Create one empty local folder called second-brain
4. Say hi to the agent; when it answers and names the folder, setup is done

Tools like Gmail, Slack and the calendar are connected later, one at a time, by the superpower that needs them.

## The six superpowers, simplest first

01. Ask your own records, not the internet — answers sourced from your files, with citations
02. The triage happens before you wake up — one skill file empties the inbox every morning
03. The reply is already written when you open it — your tone, written down once, reused forever
04. The list decides, so you stop negotiating with yourself — your ranking order, written down once
05. Produce documents and decks — gather sources, approve them, only then write
06. Every morning, a podcast made only for me — a spoken brief generated overnight

Each superpower runs as three beats: explain it, see it, build it.

## Rules that make it work

- Cite the file every fact came from. If it is not in the files, say so rather than guess.
- The agent drafts, you send. Keep that boundary on anything carrying a commitment.
- When an answer is wrong, fix the instruction rather than the answer, so it stays fixed.
- Approve the source list before a single sentence gets written.
- Do not automate the human parts. 1:1s and growth conversations stay manual, on purpose.

## The seven-day challenge

Day 1-2: document your projects and your team. Day 3-4: document your processes. Day 5-6: capture a note after every meeting. Day 7: write your first skill.

- Which of my tools is worth connecting first?
- What is the one recurring task I would most like to stop doing by hand?
